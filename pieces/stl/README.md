# Organism — print-ready STLs

The eight print-ready part meshes plus a ready-to-slice disk plate. **Units:
millimetres.** Parts generated by `../gather_stls` from the canonical repo sources
(see *Source* column); `disk_plate.stl` by `../build_disk_plate.py`.

| `MOVE.stl` | 3D MOVE piece (sculpt + connector) | 36×35 | 57.0 | see note | `out/MOVE_sculpt_graft.obj` |
| `GROW.stl` | 3D GROW piece (sculpt + connector) | 37×37 | 37.1 | yes | `out/GROW_sculpt_graft.obj` |

The **2D disks** are flat silhouette stand-ins for the sculpted pieces: universal
connector **peg (dome+ridge) on top**, matching **food socket carved into the
bottom** — so a disk pegs onto a piece/food, and food/another disk nests onto it.
Heights include the peg (≈9 mm body + ≈4 mm peg). Their socket is **self-supporting**
(the food method): a pointed cone cap + tented ridge roof, so every downward-facing
ceiling is ≤30° from vertical and it prints socket-down with **no support inside**.

Their silhouettes are sampled straight from the vector outlines at high density
(≈1600 points), so the curves are continuous — max flat-chord ≤ 0.26 mm and
curve-vs-facet deviation ≤ 8 µm, well below the 0.4 mm nozzle. (The earlier disks
tessellated the vectors coarsely: 1.4–2.7 mm chords, up to 86 µm — visibly
faceted.) All three are single watertight solids (Euler 2) with a crisp,
un-remeshed connector.

## Universal connector (all pieces share it)

Peg on top / socket on the bottom, per `connector_meta.json`:

| Piece | Socket on bottom? |
|---|---|
| EAT | no (top peg only) |
| MOVE, GROW, FOOD, all 2D disks | yes |

Anything mates with anything: pieces stack on pieces, food nests on pieces, disks
stand in for pieces.

## Print notes

- **Orientation:** peg up, socket/flat side on the bed.
- **2D disks + `disk_plate.stl`: print with supports OFF.** The socket is
  self-supporting (cone cap + tented ridge, ≤30° from vertical → 0 % of the ceiling
  needs support), and the disk bottom is flat, so nothing needs support inside or
  out. The small bottom-edge bevel (~0.7 mm, ~45°) prints fine. A brim isn't
  needed (large flat footprint) but doesn't hurt.
- **FOOD socket** (`FOOD_slip/snap.stl`, the non-self-supporting build): its cavity
  ceiling is a short overhang — **bridges cleanly in PETG**; paint a **support
  blocker** over the cavity if your slicer tries to fill it, and support the flared
  rim. (A self-supporting FOOD variant exists via `build_food_ss.py`.)
- **`MOVE.stl` (3D piece) is flagged non-watertight** but has **0 holes** — only 9
  non-manifold interior edges from the sculpt→connector union. Every slicer
  (PrusaSlicer / Bambu / Cura) resolves these on load; prints fine as-is. Left
  faithful to the canonical `MOVE_sculpt_graft.obj` rather than altered.
- **FOOD is a different filament colour** from the pieces in the game, so it
  normally prints on its own plate. Two fits provided — `slip` nests loosely,
  `snap` presses on and stays.

## Provenance / rebuilding

The 3D piece STLs are regenerated faithfully (`process=False`) from the
git-tracked sculpt-graft OBJs (`out/*_sculpt_graft.obj`, last updated in commit
`6816e80` "sculpted pieces"). FOOD and the 2D disks are byte-for-byte copies of
the current build outputs. To rebuild from scratch: `make print` (pieces + food)
and `make disks` (2D disks), then `python gather_stls.py` to refresh this dir.
`make diskplate` builds `disk_plate.stl` from the current disks.

The **2D disks** are built by `build_minimal.py`: `clean_svg.py` (repo venv —
shapely + svgelements) samples each `inputs/*.svg` outline into a dense, clean
simple polygon, which Blender extrudes + bevels + grafts the connector onto. This
replaced the old Blender SVG-importer path, whose coarse arc-fill both faceted the
curves and left GROW's trefoil non-manifold (~1000 hole edges → previously needed
a lossy voxel remesh). Now all three come out watertight and crisp with no remesh.
The socket is carved with `sor._socket_ss` (the self-supporting food profile, gap =
`gc.SOCKET_GAP`); set `MIN_SS_SOCKET=0` for the legacy flat-apex socket.
